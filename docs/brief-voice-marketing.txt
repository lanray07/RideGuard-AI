45. VOICE-FIRST RIDING EXPERIENCE — CORE FEATURE
==================================================

Voice interaction is a FLAGSHIP SAFETY FEATURE of RideGuard AI.

CORE PRINCIPLE:

“Eyes on the road. Hands on the bike.”

During an active ride, minimise the need for cyclists to touch or look at
their iPhone.

Where supported by Apple's current APIs, integrate App Intents, Siri,
Shortcuts, speech technologies, Apple Watch and audio feedback.

Do not invent unsupported Siri capabilities.

--------------------------------------------------
A. VOICE COMMANDS
--------------------------------------------------

Design supported hands-free actions for commands/intents such as:

“Report a pothole.”

“Report blocked cycle lane.”

“Report debris.”

“Report roadworks.”

“Report broken glass.”

“Report flooding.”

“Report poor lighting.”

“Report dangerous junction.”

“What's ahead?”

“What's my ETA?”

“Check me in.”

“Share my ride.”

“I'm okay.”

“End my ride.”

“Take me home.”

“Find a lower-risk route home.”

“Repeat that.”

Use App Intents / Siri / Shortcuts where technically supported.

If an interaction cannot be implemented reliably using public Apple APIs,
do not create a fake voice assistant.

--------------------------------------------------
B. VOICE HAZARD REPORTING
--------------------------------------------------

A rider should be able to report an issue with minimal interaction.

Example:

Rider:
“Report pothole.”

RideGuard:
“Pothole marked at your current location.”

Record:

• approximate/current location
• timestamp
• travel direction where available
• report category

Do not require the rider to type while moving.

After the ride, optionally ask:

“Would you like to add details to the pothole report?”

--------------------------------------------------
C. NATURAL VOICE REPORTING
--------------------------------------------------

Where a reliable speech/AI pipeline is implemented, support natural phrases:

“There's broken glass in the cycle lane.”

“Big pothole here.”

“Roadworks are blocking the bike lane.”

“There’s debris ahead.”

Classify the statement into the appropriate hazard category.

Example structured output:

category: blockedCycleLane
location: currentLocation
timestamp: currentTime
confidence: 0.94

If confidence is low, save it as requiring confirmation rather than
inventing the classification.

--------------------------------------------------
D. SPOKEN HAZARD ALERTS
--------------------------------------------------

Provide optional spoken alerts.

Examples:

“Reported pothole ahead in 200 metres.”

“Roadworks reported ahead.”

“Cycle lane obstruction reported ahead.”

“Complex junction approaching.”

Keep alerts:

• short
• calm
• actionable
• non-alarmist

Do not overwhelm riders with constant speech.

Implement alert prioritisation and cooldown logic.

--------------------------------------------------
E. “WHAT'S AHEAD?”
--------------------------------------------------

Allow:

“What's ahead?”

Example response:

“One recent roadworks report is approximately 600 metres ahead.
Your next turn is right in 400 metres.”

Only mention information supported by current route data.

--------------------------------------------------
F. VOICE ROUTE COMPARISON
--------------------------------------------------

Before beginning a ride, allow:

“Find me a lower-risk route to work.”

Example response:

“I found an alternative estimated at 28 minutes.
It's four minutes longer than the fastest option and uses more
protected cycling infrastructure.”

Only make comparisons supported by route data.

--------------------------------------------------
G. VOICE LIVE RIDE
--------------------------------------------------

Support voice-accessible Live Ride actions.

Examples:

“Start Live Ride.”

“Share this ride with Sarah.”

“What's my ETA?”

“Tell Sarah I'm running late.”

“I'm okay.”

“Check me in.”

“End Live Ride.”

Sensitive actions must use appropriate confirmation.

--------------------------------------------------
H. OVERDUE CHECK-IN VOICE RESPONSE
--------------------------------------------------

If RideGuard detects that a ride has exceeded its expected arrival window:

Spoken prompt where technically appropriate:

“Your ride is taking longer than expected. Are you okay?”

Provide extremely simple responses:

“I’m okay.”

“Need more time.”

“End ride.”

“Emergency.”

Never infer an accident merely because the cyclist is late.

--------------------------------------------------
I. EMERGENCY VOICE ACCESS
--------------------------------------------------

Provide rapid access to system-supported emergency functionality.

Potential commands/intents:

“Emergency.”

“Call emergency services.”

“Contact my emergency person.”

“Share my location.”

Use native Apple/system capabilities wherever possible.

RideGuard must NEVER claim that it is itself an emergency-response service.

--------------------------------------------------
J. APPLE WATCH VOICE EXPERIENCE
--------------------------------------------------

Voice should be especially prominent on Apple Watch.

Allow supported interactions for:

Report Hazard

What's Ahead?

ETA

I'm Okay

Check In

Combine:

• voice

Minimise visual interaction.

--------------------------------------------------
L. DISTRACTION SAFETY
--------------------------------------------------

The voice system must follow this rule:

RIDING MODE = MINIMAL INTERACTION.

Do not create lengthy conversations during active rides.

Avoid:

long AI answers
complex menus
multiple questions
advertising
paywall interruptions
unnecessary notifications

During active cycling:

VOICE → SHORT ANSWER → ACTION → RETURN TO RIDE

--------------------------------------------------
M. VOICE ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------------------------------

Create a modular service layer such as:

RideVoiceService

VoiceIntentRouter

SpokenAlertManager

HazardVoiceClassifier

RideAudioSessionManager

Potential functions:

reportHazardByVoice()

classifyHazardSpeech()

announceHazard()

announceNextTurn()

announceETA()

announceRouteComparison()

startLiveRide()

sendCheckIn()

respondToOverduePrompt()

endRide()

Use deterministic application logic for actions.

AI can classify or explain language but should not control
safety-critical actions without validation.

--------------------------------------------------
N. PRIVACY
--------------------------------------------------

Voice data is sensitive.

Clearly disclose:

• whether audio leaves the device
• whether transcription occurs on-device
• whether transcripts are stored
• whether an AI service receives transcripts

Prefer on-device processing when technically appropriate.

Do not retain raw audio unnecessarily.

Provide deletion controls.
